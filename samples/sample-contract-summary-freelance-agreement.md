## The contract (synthetic, summarised)

A freelance services agreement between **Asha Verma** (freelancer,
a fictional person) and **BrightLeaf Studio** (client, a fictional
company), dated 1 November 2026. In plain terms:

1. **Work:** Asha will design a logo and a landing page for
   BrightLeaf Studio, delivering two rounds of revisions.
2. **Fee:** ₹40,000, half on signing and half on delivery.
3. **Deadline:** delivery by 30 November 2026.
4. **Ownership:** the designs belong to BrightLeaf Studio once the
   final fee is paid; until then, Asha keeps the rights.
5. **Late delivery:** if Asha delivers late, BrightLeaf Studio may
   deduct ₹1,000 per day of delay, up to ₹10,000.
6. **Cancellation:** either side may cancel with 7 days' written
   notice; work already done is paid for.

## Validity check — Indian Contract Act, 1872, Section 10

Citation: Indian Contract Act, 1872, Sections 2(h) and 10.
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Section 10 provides that all agreements are contracts if they are
made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a
lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not expressly
declared to be void.

Applied to this synthetic agreement:

| Essential (s. 10) | Check |
|---|---|
| Free consent | Both parties signed; nothing in the text suggests coercion, fraud or misrepresentation. (A real check would ask how the agreement was actually made.) |
| Competent parties | Both are stated to be adults of sound mind. (A real check would verify age and capacity.) |
| Lawful consideration | ₹40,000 for design work — lawful. |
| Lawful object | Designing a logo and a landing page — lawful. |
| Not expressly void | Nothing in the agreement is of a kind the Act declares void. |

**Result:** on its face, the synthetic agreement meets the five

## If the contract is broken — Indian Contract Act, 1872, Section 73

Citation: Indian Contract Act, 1872, Section 73.
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Section 73 provides that when a contract has been broken, the party
who suffers is entitled to compensation for loss or damage which
naturally arose in the usual course of things from the breach, or
which the parties knew, when they made the contract, was likely to
result from it. Compensation is not given for remote and indirect
loss.

Applied to this synthetic agreement:

- **Late delivery:** the ₹1,000-per-day deduction (capped at
  ₹10,000) is the parties' own agreed measure. If BrightLeaf Studio
  claimed more than the cap, s. 73 would ask whether the extra loss
  naturally arose from the delay or was known to be likely when the
  contract was made — and would refuse remote or indirect loss.
- **Non-payment:** if BrightLeaf Studio did not pay the final
  ₹20,000, Asha's loss would be the unpaid fee — loss that naturally
  arises from non-payment.
- **Cancellation:** the 7-day notice clause governs; work already
  done is paid for, which matches the s. 73 idea that compensation
  follows the loss actually caused.
