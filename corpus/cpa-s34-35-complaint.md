# Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — Sections 34–35: Jurisdiction and who may file a complaint

Act: Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act 35 of 2019)
Section: Sections 34–35 (Chapter IV — Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)
Source: https://www.aaptaxlaw.com/consumer-protection-act/section-34-jurisdiction-of-district-commission (Gazette of India, No. 35 of 2019)

## Statutory text (summary of the provisions, verified)

**Section 34 — Jurisdiction of the District Commission.** A District
Commission has jurisdiction where the value of the goods or services
paid as consideration does not exceed the prescribed limit. The Act's
text sets that limit at ₹1 crore; the Consumer Protection
(Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and
the National Commission) Rules, 2021 prescribe the operative limit
of **₹50 lakh**. The territorial jurisdiction (s. 34(2)) covers the
place where: the opposite party resides or carries on business, or
personally works for gain; or the cause of action, wholly or in part,
arises; or the complainant resides or personally works for gain.

**Section 35 — Manner of filing.** A complaint may be filed by:
(a) the consumer; (b) any recognised consumer association; (c) one
or more consumers where there are numerous consumers having the same
interest, with the permission of the District Commission; (d) the
Central Government or any State Government; or (e) the Central
Authority. A complaint may be filed **electronically**.

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

A consumer complaint goes to the District Commission if the value
paid is within ₹50 lakh (the operative limit under the 2021 Rules).
You can file where you live or work — you do not have to travel to
where the seller is. You can file online. The State Commission
handles values above the District Commission limit, and the National
Commission handles the highest values. A refund is the most common
consumer complaint: if a seller refuses a refund for a defective
product — or has refused one — that is a complaint under the Act.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The practical answer to "where and how do I complain?" — read with
s. 2(7) (are you a consumer?) and s. 69 (the two-year limit).
