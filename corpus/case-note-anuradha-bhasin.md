## Case note (verified)

In *Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India* (2020) 3 SCC 637, the
Supreme Court held that **access to the internet is protected under
Article 19(1)(a)** (freedom of speech and expression). Any
restriction on internet access — including a shutdown — must satisfy
the **test of proportionality**: it must be necessary, and no wider
than needed to achieve its aim.

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

This case is why a blanket internet shutdown is a constitutional
question, not just an administrative order: the internet is where
speech happens, so switching it off restricts speech, and the
restriction must be proportionate.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The bridge between Article 19(1)(a) and online life — read with
Article 19(2) (the eight permitted grounds of restriction) and the
*Shreya Singhal* case note.
