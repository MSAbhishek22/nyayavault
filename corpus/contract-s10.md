# Indian Contract Act, 1872 — Sections 2(h) and 10: What is a contract

Act: Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act 9 of 1872)
Section: Sections 2(h) and 10 (Chapter I — Of the communication, acceptance and revocation of proposals)
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (India Code); bare Act text at https://bnblegal.com/indian-contract-act-1872/

#contract #essentials #agreement #validity

## Statutory text (verbatim extract)

Section 2(h) — "contract":

> "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract."

Section 10 — "What agreements are contracts":

> "All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void."

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

A contract is an agreement that the law will enforce. Section 10
lists the five essentials: (1) free consent — no coercion, undue
influence, fraud, misrepresentation or mistake; (2) parties
competent to contract — of sound mind and of majority age
(18 years); (3) lawful consideration — something of value given in
return; (4) lawful object — the purpose must not be forbidden,
immoral, or against public policy; (5) not expressly declared void
by the Act. The second paragraph of s. 10 adds that this section
does not affect any law requiring an agreement to be in writing, or
to be attested, or to be registered.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The validity check for any contract — the five essentials are the
checklist the contract summary sample runs against a real agreement.
