# Constitution of India — Article 32: Remedies for enforcement of rights conferred by this Part

Act: Constitution of India, 1950
Section: Article 32 (Part III — Right to Constitutional Remedies)
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (India Code); https://sooperkanoon.com/act/133326/constitution-of-india-complete-act

#constitution #fundamental-rights #remedies #writs

## Statutory text (what the Article does)

Article 32 gives citizens the right to move the Supreme Court for
the enforcement of the Fundamental Rights in Part III, through the
five writs: **habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, and
quo warranto**. Dr B. R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "heart and
soul" of the Constitution.

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right — the right to a remedy.
If a Fundamental Right is violated, the person affected can go
directly to the Supreme Court. The five writs are the court's tools:
habeas corpus (produce a detained person), mandamus (order a public
authority to do its duty), prohibition (stop a lower court or
tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction), certiorari (quash a lower
decision), quo warranto (ask by what authority a person holds a
public office).

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The answer to "where do I go if my fundamental right is violated?"
— the enforcement route behind Articles 14, 19 and 21.
