# Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — Section 69: Limitation period

Act: Consumer Protection Act, 2019 (Act 35 of 2019)
Section: Section 69 (Chapter IV — Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)
Source: https://www.soolegal.com/bare-acts/consumer-protection-act-2019 (Gazette of India, No. 35 of 2019)

#consumer-protection #limitation #two-years #complaint

## Statutory text (summary of the provision, verified)

Section 69 provides that the District Commission, State Commission
or National Commission **shall not admit a complaint unless it is
filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action
has arisen**. Where there has been delay, the Commission may
condone it — that is, admit the complaint filed late — if it is
satisfied that there was **sufficient cause** for not filing within
the two-year period.

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

A consumer complaint has a two-year deadline, counted from when the
problem happened (the cause of action). Missing the deadline does
not always end the case — the Commission can accept a late complaint
if you show a good reason for the delay — but you must explain the
delay and satisfy the Commission. Filing early is always safer.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The deadline answer for any consumer complaint — read with ss. 34–35
(where and how to file) and s. 2(7) (are you a consumer?).
