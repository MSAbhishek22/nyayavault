# Information Technology Act, 2000 — Section 66D: Punishment for cheating by personation by using computer resource

Act: Information Technology Act, 2000 (Act 21 of 2000)
Section: Section 66D (Chapter XI — Offences)
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (India Code); bare Act text at https://www.courtkutchehry.com/bare-acts/information-technology-act-2000/

#it-act #cyber #cheating #personation #impersonation #fraud

## Statutory text (verbatim extract)

> "Whoever, by means of any communication device or computer resource cheats by personating, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years and shall also be liable to fine which may extend to one lakh rupees."

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

Section 66D is the cheating-by-personation section: pretending to
be someone else — a bank officer, a friend, a company — through a
phone, computer or other device, in order to cheat. The punishment
is imprisonment up to three years and a fine up to ₹1 lakh. It
pairs with s. 66C: 66C covers the *use* of another person's
identifier, 66D covers the *cheating* done while personating. A
common pattern is a stolen password used to take money from a bank
account: that is identity theft under s. 66C and cheating by
personation under s. 66D.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The core provision for phishing, fake-customer-care and
"pretending to be someone else online to cheat" — read with s. 66C
and s. 43.
