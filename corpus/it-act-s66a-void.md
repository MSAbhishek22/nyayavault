# Information Technology Act, 2000 — Section 66A: VOID (struck down)

Act: Information Technology Act, 2000 (Act 21 of 2000)
Section: Section 66A — STRUCK DOWN, VOID, NOT LIVE LAW
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (India Code); Supreme Court judgment, Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) 5 SCC 1

#it-act #case-note #void #shreya-singhal #free-speech

## Case note (verified) — this is NOT a live provision

**Section 66A was struck down in its entirety by the Supreme Court
in *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India* (2015) 5 SCC 1**, as
unconstitutional — it violated Article 19(1)(a) (freedom of speech
and expression).

Section 66A used to punish sending, through a computer resource or
communication device, information that was "grossly offensive",
menacing, or known to be false, for the purpose of causing
annoyance, inconvenience, danger, obstruction, insult, injury,
criminal intimidation, enmity, hatred or ill will. The Court held
that the section was vague and overbroad: it covered speech that
Article 19(2) does not permit restricting.

**Section 66A is void and unenforceable.** It must never be cited
as current law, and a person cannot be prosecuted under it.

## Why this note exists in the corpus

So the app can answer "is s. 66A still valid?" correctly — **no** —
instead of hallucinating it as live law. A provision's death is as
important a fact as its text.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The bridge between Article 19 and the IT Act: Article 19(1)(a) is
the right, Article 19(2) is the list of permitted restrictions, and
*Shreya Singhal* is the case that shows what happens when a law goes
beyond that list.
