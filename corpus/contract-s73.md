# Indian Contract Act, 1872 — Section 73: Compensation for loss or damage caused by breach of contract

Act: Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act 9 of 1872)
Section: Section 73 (Chapter VI — Of the consequences of breach of contract)
Source: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/ (India Code); bare Act text at https://bnblegal.com/indian-contract-act-1872/

#contract #breach #damages #compensation

## Statutory text (verbatim extract, first paragraph)

> "When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken the contract, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the party knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it. Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach."

## Plain-language note (summary, not the law)

Section 73 is the damages rule. If a contract is broken, the
suffering party is entitled to compensation — but only for loss that
(1) **naturally arose in the usual course of things** from the
breach, or (2) the parties **knew, when they made the contract**,
was likely to result from a breach. **Remote and indirect loss is
not compensated.** The section also states (second paragraph) that
compensation is due for failure to discharge obligations resembling
a contract, and (third paragraph) that in estimating the loss, the
means available to remedy the inconvenience caused by the breach
must be taken into account.

## Why this provision is in the corpus

The damages answer for any broken contract — the "what can I claim"
provision, read with s. 10 (was the contract valid at all?).
